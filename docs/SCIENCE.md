# Historical interpretation

## Original detections

MODIS Terra/Aqua measures thermal anomalies at nominal 1 km resolution. VIIRS S-NPP I-band pixels have nominal 375 m resolution. Pixel centers can include industrial heat, offshore sources, volcanoes and vegetation burning. Repeated detections of a source are expected. FRP is instantaneous pixel-integrated radiative power in MW. Counts do not measure incidents, duration or burned area. Archive type is an inferred class. NRT type is unknown. All source classes remain in scope. Archive and NRT can differ in processing and latency.

## Shared grid

Layer A counts supplied rows before quality exclusions. For Layer B, require valid WGS84 coordinates, UTC day and HHMM, expected platform/instrument, and documented confidence domain. Use the nominal/high VIIRS classes and a declared MODIS confidence cutoff of 30 on its own numeric scale. This analyst policy does not equate the scales. Use original decimal coordinates to compute west/south-inclusive 0.02° bins anchored at (−180°, −90°). At the global maximum use the last cell. A north/south side is about 2.2 km. Its east/west width varies with latitude. The grid is neither equal-area nor a measurement of fire extent.

Count retained observations within each (UTC day, cell, sensor). Count the MODIS-only, VIIRS-only and shared cell-days separately. Union counts each occupied cell once that day. Summing days preserves repeat activity across dates. Deduplication matches all shared measurement fields, including exact location, time, platform, scan, track, thermal bands, confidence, day/night and FRP. Exact within-product copies require matching version and type too. Across overlapping archive and NRT a matching standard record takes precedence. A location/time coincidence alone never removes a row. Raw counts retain copies. The grid mitigates repeat sampling, while orbit, resolution, obscuration, overpass time and persistent sources still affect the record.

## Measures, sensor and area

Raw Integrated sums the two sensor row counts. Occupied MODIS cell-days equal MODIS-only plus shared cell-days; VIIRS cell-days equal VIIRS-only plus shared cell-days. Integrated adds the three disjoint classes. Before the first S-NPP date, the Lahore full envelope has 2,585 raw MODIS observations and 2,283 occupied cell-days. California has 114,055 and 61,653 respectively. For each area the integrated occupancy exactly equals the MODIS occupancy in that period.

Custom rectangles include every original point center satisfying west ≤ longitude ≤ east and south ≤ latitude ≤ north, using the supplied precision. Quality-filtered cell occupancy is computed after this spatial selection so moving a boundary can change a shared cell to a single-sensor cell. Raw counts include confidence exclusions and duplicate rows. Only unique quality-retained measurements establish occupancy. Published points retain raw membership and the retention flag. Actual source validation found no invalid, unlocatable records in these files. A region's pooled observed-point envelope does not verify equal sensor footprints or complete spatial coverage. Small areas can contain real records without a sampled map marker.

## Coverage and comparisons

The readmes link to a generic FIRMS description. Request dates, exact requested footprints, overpass completeness, masks and cloud/smoke obscuration are unknown. Both products may have different effective footprints inside the pooled observed envelope. The first and last positive dates limit the interface, without verifying coverage between them. A silent day gets an unknown state. Dates outside NASA FIRMS's stated S-NPP temporal coverage (20 January 2012–2 November 2026) are unavailable. Edge dates/months/years and sensor transitions are marked. A zero retained count following raw detections indicates quality exclusion. A verified query zero would require independent request evidence. These inputs provide none.

For each region, report paired positive grid months and years, then require independently documented, comparable month support for both instruments, at least six complete supported years and positive pairs across seasons. A comparison month also needs matching platform configuration and temporal support. If eligible, fit the median VIIRS/MODIS occupied-cell-day ratio on earlier years; hold out the last two whole years, compare mean absolute error with the unscaled MODIS baseline, report seasonal residuals and the training-ratio interquartile interval. Require lower overall error, no worse seasonal MAE, and a ratio IQR no larger than its median. These are explicit screening choices, not NASA calibration standards. Failure withholds an index. No coefficient transfers between regions. The historical index is withheld for unverified months. A separate retrospective sensor-agreement diagnostic uses only archive months with positive recorded occupancy from both sensors. See the [scientific review](REVIEW.md) for its fixed year split, seasonal checks and limitations.

Same-month anomaly assessment requires at least five independently supported, complete reference years in the same instrument era, excluding the target year. Compare to the median and midrank percentile. With unknown support the workstation instead plots recorded historical values with sample size, marks gaps and partial periods, matches day-of-month bounds and instrument era, and withholds historical peak rankings and anomaly claims. Calendar month/season comparisons can reflect changing sampling intensity. Seasons shown are northern meteorological seasons.

A descriptive same-month median uses the current rectangle, selected class, measure and sensor. Target and peers must have positive standard-product detections from each active selected sensor on every matched calendar day, with the same era, Aqua start configuration and no flagged MODIS gap interval. At least five other years must pass. This intentionally selects well-recorded, potentially high-activity periods. The median describes recorded activity only. It does not correct for cloud, overpass effort, product changes or fire sensitivity.

During daily drilldown the reference matches the exact UTC month and day in other years within the date filter. Exports match each row's own inclusive date bounds, including clipped months and single-day monthly exports. Single-sensor MODIS comparisons follow Terra/Aqua availability without imposing the unrelated S-NPP start date. Integrated comparisons retain the S-NPP era split. The archive has no verified daily completeness schedule. Missing rows stay unknown, and February 29 never matches February 28. Each ZIP comment and member comment was empty, and the four embedded readmes link only to the generic FIRMS product readme.

## Conditional reference distributions

The historical panel now calculates a distribution for the exact selected calendar dates, area, classification, metric and sensor configuration. **Other eligible years** excludes the target year and is explicitly retrospective. **Earlier eligible years only** also excludes later years, avoiding future-record leakage when reviewing a past period. Both remain inside the visible date filter and retain all existing daily archive-support rules. Exact month/day matching conditions on calendar seasonality without pooling different seasons or sensor eras.

With at least five eligible reference years and an eligible target, report the median, linearly interpolated empirical quartiles (Hyndman–Fan type 7), 10th/90th percentiles, and the target's empirical midrank percentile: `100 × (# below + 0.5 × # equal) / N`. Report the signed deviation `target − median`. Relative percentage is withheld when the median is zero. These percentile ranges describe the observed reference sample, not prediction intervals or population probabilities.

With at least eight eligible reference years and a positive median absolute deviation, report **robust deviation = (target − median) / (1.4826 × MAD)**, where `MAD = median(|reference − median(reference)|)`. The requested scale factor gives a familiar robust spread convention; it does not establish normality, independence, a p-value or calibrated risk. The original unscaled `(target − median) / MAD` remains in CSV as `recorded_mad_units` for compatibility; the scaled quantity is distinctly named `recorded_robust_deviation`. Constant/sparse references produce an unavailable score, never infinity or a fabricated zero. Sample minima are project cautionary choices, not guarantees of stationarity. [NIST discussion of outlier assumptions and robust deviations](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/eda/section3/eda35h.htm)

For Lahore on 5 November 2021, original CSVs independently give 57 cell-days, median 22, Q25–Q75 11–25.5, MAD 8, unscaled deviation/MAD 4.375 and **scaled robust deviation 2.951** across eight other eligible years. The empirical percentile is 100, meaning above those eight observed references, not a 100% risk or significance claim. Earlier-years mode, now the public default, has six eligible references, median 22 and Q25–Q75 13–25; the score and unusualness label are withheld. Both modes remain conditional on positive daily archive detections, a selection that favors activity.

Public labels require an eligible target, at least eight references and nonzero MAD. “Higher than usual recorded activity” requires midrank percentile ≥90 and scaled deviation ≥2; the lower label requires percentile ≤10 and deviation ≤−2. The central interquartile ranks receive “Typical recorded activity”; remaining supported values are labeled above/below the recorded median. These thresholds define descriptive labels only, with no claimed false-alarm rate, population significance or wildfire-warning interpretation. Sparse history and zero spread have explicit unavailable messages. Target exclusion, earlier-year isolation, ties, leap dates and raw-versus-cell units are tested.

Record-support counters separately report in-range selected sensor-days with detections, without detections, with NRT, and with raw detections but zero retained observations. Sensor-days without records remain **unknown**, not verified zeros or proven missed acquisitions. A completed archive query, if documented later, would establish a query result, not clear-sky satellite completeness. Calendar/date-filter clipping identifies partial intervals separately. Missing static files cause a load error rather than fabricated empty data.

The [extended transfer audit](TRANSFER_VALIDATION.md) tests additional seasonal and geographic models and a genuinely separate retained-detection-count target. It changes no source observations or grid counts.

## Seasonal comparison denominators

Seasonal bars remain **descriptive cumulative totals over the full selected date filter**, independent of month/day drilldown. Each bar now shows the number of selected calendar-month windows and how many are clipped; its accessible description reports selected calendar days. The analytic function also reports detection-positive days/months, but these are not treated as exposure or displayed as rates. Unknown days contribute no recorded count without becoming verified zeros. A quality-filtered zero is shown only when source detections exist; otherwise the season says “No record”.

Dividing by detection-positive months would condition the denominator on the measured outcome. Dividing by calendar days would yield a recorded-total/calendar-time ratio but still would not correct missing acquisitions or sensor sensitivity. Neither supports an unbiased seasonal intensity comparison here, so no normalized seasonal rate is published. The chart explicitly labels unequal calendar lengths and missing-record limitations. A hand-checked leap-year fixture verifies calendar-day/month counts, partial intervals, known quality zeros and unknown dates; both complete regional seasonal sums reconcile to the unchanged Integrated totals.

The public explorer retains concise point-of-use context and supported labels. Source audits, overlap components, calibration error tables, acceptance results and detailed coverage counters remain in repository documentation and scientific exports rather than interrupting exploration.

## Reference eligibility audit

`referencePeriods` records each candidate year's date-range, calendar-date and sensor-configuration exclusions separately from positive daily archive support. `sameMonthSeries` exposes only matching calendar/configuration windows to the existing descriptive chart. Support diagnostics count required days, days with source detections and days containing NRT for each active selected sensor; these are record counts, never verified observation exposure. The unchanged daily archive rule requires a positive standard-only record from each active selected sensor on every matched day and no historical gap advisory.

The reference audit additionally excludes the target itself and, under earlier-years policy, later years. Eligibility does not depend on raw versus cell units; values do. Original points are filtered by AOI/classification before both values and support are computed. A clipped calendar interval can be compared to the same dates in other years; an unclipped month can still have unknown record support. An observed raw record that fails quality filtering can establish a recorded zero in retained occupancy, without proving no burning.

Target NRT, missing daily records, unavailable sensors and insufficient reference N now have distinct messages and export reasons. An unsupported target does not mean eligible historical peers are absent. No threshold was weakened and no missing date was imputed. The [October audit](REVIEW.md#october-reference-correction) lists the actual years and independent source-derived checks. These references remain conditional on positive records and cannot validate formal anomaly significance or physical sensor equivalence.

## Identifiable quantities and missing evidence

| Estimand | What the existing observations identify | What they do not identify |
|---|---|---|
| A. Common-grid recorded activity | Exact selected-source MODIS/VIIRS occupancy, intersection, union and cell-days after the declared filters | Missed burning, burned area or physical fire counts |
| B. Sensor-specific detection activity | Same-place/calendar-period observed counts, signed differences, conditional MAE and Jaccard | Stable detection-probability correction or equivalent clear-sky sampling |
| C. Sensor-independent latent burning activity | No unique estimate | Undetected activity, per-sensor detection probabilities and false-positive rates without further evidence |

**Constructive non-identifiability.** Even grant ideal assumptions more favorable than these files support: perfect specificity, a common grid/time population and known associations. Let a, b and c be observed MODIS-only, VIIRS-only and jointly detected cell-days; U = a + b + c. Let h be an unknown count missed by both sensors. For any geometrically possible h ≥ 0, a latent population L = U + h and four detection-pattern proportions

`q10 = a/L; q01 = b/L; q11 = c/L; q00 = h/L`

are nonnegative, sum to one, and yield the identical observed counts `(L q10, L q01, L q11) = (a, b, c)`. Therefore the positive-only table does not determine h or L. Jaccard `c/U` also remains unchanged. The proportions are a mathematical construction, **not estimated probabilities or invented observations**. Under a conditional Poisson count model the observed likelihood likewise depends only on these three observed intensities; the unobserved fourth intensity is unconstrained. Assuming sensor independence could close this system, but shared obscuration, changing sensitivity and mismatched overpasses do not justify it. Actual uncertain fire classification makes inference to burning still weaker.

The exact-rational regression test uses authentic 2024–2025 tables: California `(a,b,c) = (5,396, 25,227, 5,605)` and Lahore `(240, 1,710, 62)`. Algebraic choices h = 0 and h = U reproduce each observed table exactly while implying different latent totals. Neither choice is an estimate or calibration. This establishes a structural obstacle that more flexible regression or another split of the same detections cannot resolve.

The source confidence fields are not measured probabilities of detecting all fires. IGNIS retains separate numeric MODIS and categorical VIIRS rules, rather than treating them as equivalent probabilities or inverse-probability weights. NASA's [FIRMS detection notes](https://firms.modaps.eosdis.nasa.gov/alerts/notes.php) caution that MODIS confidence can vary in interpretation geographically. The README's satellite-limitations section distinguishes these NASA-documented product limitations from IGNIS's own failed calibration experiments.

**Minimum additional evidence, investigated as documentation only:**

| Missing component | Required information and purpose |
|---|---|
| Request provenance | Original FIRMS receipt, requested dates/extents, product/version and successful retrieval; establishes export scope, not observation completeness |
| Observation opportunities | Actual granule inventory, per-pixel geolocation/footprints, acquisition times, scan/view geometry and processing QA for both sensors across the selected area |
| Valid nondetection denominator | Collection-matched Level-2 MOD14/MYD14 and VNP14IMG fire masks/QA, retaining non-fire, cloud, unclassified and unprocessed states; aggregate only valid opportunities into common space/time windows |
| Sensitivity and false detections | Independent, time-resolved reference fire/thermal-source observations with documented detection limits and classification, spanning small/large sources, seasons and both regions |
| Final confirmation | A frozen method tested on genuinely unexamined years/regions with comparable opportunities; the previously inspected 2024–2025 years cannot become blind again |

NASA's [MODIS C6/C6.1 product guide](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2023-09/MODIS_C6_C6.1_Fire_User_Guide_1.0.pdf) describes full fire-mask classes, unlike the positive-point CSVs. The [NASA VNP14IMG file specification](https://ladsweb.modaps.eosdis.nasa.gov/filespec/VIIRS/1/VNP14IMG.fs) illustrates cloud, clear-land, unprocessed and confidence classes; this is an older Collection 1 specification and the exact Collection 2 schema must be verified before any future ingestion. Current catalog entries establish [MOD14 Collection 6.1](https://ladsweb.modaps.eosdis.nasa.gov/missions-and-measurements/products/MOD14/) and [VNP14IMG Collection 2](https://ladsweb.modaps.eosdis.nasa.gov/missions-and-measurements/products/VNP14IMG/). No such granules, masks or external reference observations were downloaded or incorporated. A valid non-fire pixel means the algorithm did not detect a qualifying fire at that overpass, not that no fire occurred at any time that day.

Area-normalizing unequal-angle grid counts does not turn them into burned-area measurements. Even an exact geodesic area and calendar-duration denominator would describe **recorded occupancy per unit area/time**, conditional on unknown sampling, not unbiased regional burning intensity. Parent rectangles have different land cover, class mixtures and effective footprints. The existing seasonal totals and their calendar support remain the defensible descriptive presentation.

## Statistical uncertainty and selection effects

Reference membership requires daily positive archive detections and therefore selects on observed activity. Same-date matching and earlier-year exclusion avoid season mixing and future leakage but do not restore exchangeability, equal observation effort or stationarity. Exploring many months, days, classes and AOIs also introduces selection and multiple-comparison effects. Neither the public percentile nor scaled MAD deviation is a p-value, significance level or error-controlled anomaly detector.

The empirical midrank can equal 100 with just eight references. Even under a hypothetical exchangeable, continuous null, the smallest one-sided rank-test tail probability with eight references plus one target would be 1/9 ≈ 11.1%, not zero. Those assumptions are unverified here, so IGNIS does not calculate that probability as an anomaly statistic. Ties further reduce rank resolution. No p-value, false-discovery correction or confidence interval for latent burning is published. Empirical quartiles remain descriptive ranges; year-block calibration intervals remain conditional resampling summaries with only two final test years. Nested tuning or Poisson/robust regression would not supply the missing observation process or a new independent holdout, so no additional model search was performed.
